# Initial Review Checklist: New Copyright Complaint

A reusable intake checklist for a complaint that someone has used a copyright work without permission. Work through it before forming any view on infringement. Tick each item, record the answer and the source, and note gaps.

> One section is filled in as an **EXAMPLE** using the fictional Quorvane scenario. Delete the example before use.

## A. Intake

- [ ] Date complaint received; who received it; how (email, call, meeting).
- [ ] Who is complaining, and in what capacity (author, owner, licensee, agent, employee of any of these)?
- [ ] Conflict check against the complainant, the alleged infringer and any third party named.
- [ ] What outcome does the complainant want (removal, payment, licence, apology, injunction)? Record it in their words.
- [ ] Any deadline already running (limitation, a launch date, a scheduled event)?

## B. The work

- [ ] Identify the work precisely (title, reference number, date of creation, date and place of first publication).
- [ ] Category of work (literary, dramatic, musical, artistic including photograph, cinematograph film, sound recording).
- [ ] Is it original? Note the creative choices claimed.
- [ ] Is it a derivative work or does it include third-party material (stock images, fonts, music, models' likenesses)?
- [ ] Is the original file or physical original available? Is the camera original or source file available?
- [ ] Is the work registered with the Copyright Office? (Registration is voluntary; the register is prima facie evidence of its particulars.)

## C. Title and authority

- [ ] Who created it? Employee, freelancer, commissioned, collaborator?
- [ ] Any contract, engagement email or purchase order covering rights? Obtain it.
- [ ] Any assignment? Check it is in writing, signed, and identifies the work, rights, duration and territory.
- [ ] Any licence granted by the complainant to others, including the alleged infringer? Obtain every version.
- [ ] Does any credit line or metadata name someone other than the complainant?
- [ ] If the complainant is a licensee: is the licence exclusive and in writing?

## D. The alleged infringement

- [ ] What was used, where, when and by whom? Get URLs, product names, dates.
- [ ] How was the complaint discovered? Who has seen the use?
- [ ] Is the use still continuing? Capture now if so, following the evidence protocol.
- [ ] Was the alleged infringer ever licensed? On what terms and for what period?
- [ ] Is the later work an exact copy, an edited copy, or a similar work made separately?
- [ ] Did the alleged infringer have access to the work? How do we know?

## E. Evidence handling

- [ ] Open an evidence register (`templates/evidence-register.csv`) and assign IDs.
- [ ] Preserve originals before anything else; never annotate originals.
- [ ] Record capture date and time in IST with the time zone stated.
- [ ] Hash every original file at capture or receipt.
- [ ] Identify personal data in each item; restrict access and plan redaction.
- [ ] Confirm all collection is lawful (no unauthorised access, pretexting or deceptive purchases without legal advice).

## F. Defences and risks to note early

- [ ] Licence (express or implied) or consent.
- [ ] Fair dealing or another statutory exception.
- [ ] Idea, fact or stock element rather than expression.
- [ ] Independent creation or common source.
- [ ] Delay, acquiescence or prior dealings.
- [ ] Complainant's own weak points (title gaps, overstated credits, missing originals).

## G. Procedure and proportionality

- [ ] Likely forum and the complainant's place of business.
- [ ] Likely value of the claim; is it a commercial dispute of specified value?
- [ ] Is pre-institution mediation required? Is any urgency genuine?
- [ ] Limitation: date of first infringing act; is the use continuing?
- [ ] Does the cost of each option fit the value and the client's goals?

## H. Output

- [ ] Short note to the supervising lawyer: facts, gaps, first view, recommended next steps.
- [ ] List of points needing a qualified lawyer's judgment.
