# Comparative Work Analysis

> **FICTIONAL EXERCISE. NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** The works are invented and described in words only. No image is reproduced.

## Method

1. Identify each expressive element of the Work that a photographer controls: subject selection, arrangement, props, angle, light, colour treatment, crop and timing.
2. Record what is observable in each later work, citing the evidence item the observation comes from.
3. Characterise each element as **protectable expression**, **idea**, **fact** or **stock element** (a conventional device used widely in the genre).
4. Weigh significance. Shared protectable expression points to copying; shared ideas and stock elements do not. A combination of individually conventional choices can still be protectable if the combination is the photographer's own [INFERENCE].

Limits: Observations about the pouch come from one retail unit photographed by Quorvane (E-09) and from Pebblequay's low-resolution outtakes (E-11). No calibrated overlay or expert comparison has been done. Colour and size are affected by printing.

**Reading:**
- Shared elements: subject, base, angle, crescent of seven, halved feature piece with pistachio, warm tone [SOURCED].
- Of these, only the crescent of seven together with the halved, garnished piece at the same end looks like a specific creative choice rather than a convention [INFERENCE].
- The differences (vessel, leaf direction, garnish, light direction, pouch in frame, crop) are the kind a deliberate reshoot would make, but also the kind an independent photographer would produce [INFERENCE].
- Applying *R.G. Anand*: an ordinary viewer comparing the two may notice a family resemblance. Whether they would get an "unmistakable impression" of copying is doubtful on the present descriptions [INFERENCE].
- Pebblequay's prior access (E-06) makes coincidence less likely for the shared combination, but access plus similarity in conventional elements is not enough on its own [CONDITIONAL].

**Conclusion on Table B:** Indeterminate. The comparison should be repeated on a high-resolution scan of the pouch and on Pebblequay's original shoot files. A brief or mood board showing that the Work was used as a reference would be highly significant either way.
