# Limitations

> **FICTIONAL EXERCISE. NOT LEGAL ADVICE.**

## 1. Limitations of the scenario

- The dispute, parties, works and evidence are invented. Real files are messier: more documents, more inconsistencies, and witnesses whose accounts change.
- The Work is described in words only. A real comparison of photographs must be done visually, ideally with expert help and calibrated overlays.
- Facts were designed to create specific legal questions. A real dispute may turn on issues not built into this scenario, such as moral rights, the respondent's insurance, or commercial pressures on either side.
- The respondent's internal facts (shoot files, brief, sales) are known only through its own assertions.

## 2. Limitations of the research

- **Date of research:** completed on 30 October 2026, which is also the assumed date of analysis. Later developments are not reflected.
- **Sources:** I relied on official sources where I could reach them (India Code, the Copyright Office website, the Supreme Court's judgment archive, a Delhi High Court judgment page). Where I could not, I used reproductions on unofficial databases or secondary commentary, and marked the authority accordingly. Full details are in `research/sources-and-case-law.md`.
- **Authorities not fully verified (and how the analysis treats them):**
  - *Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak*: holding seen only in part. Used for a general originality standard that is not decisive here.
  - *Arjun Panditrao Khotkar*: operative conclusion only. Used for background; its fit with s. 63 BSA is flagged as unresolved.
  - *Patil Automation*: mandatory nature verified in the text; the effective-date point is from secondary sources only.
  - *Yamini Manohar*: seen only as quoted by the Delhi High Court and in commentary.
  - *MySpace v. Super Cassettes*: secondary sources only. Used only in the optional platform-takedown option.
  - *Temple Island Collections*: English persuasive authority; header of the approved judgment seen, holding from commentary. Used only to frame a question.
  - Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, s. 170 and the Schedule; Limitation Act, 1963, Article 88 and s. 22; DPDP Act, 2023, s. 17(1)(a) and the DPDP Rules, 2025 commencement; IT Rules, 2021, rule 3(1)(d) as amended in 2025: seen in reproductions or secondary sources, not in the official consolidated text.
- **Not researched at all:**
  - Information Technology Act, 2000, s. 79 (text not inspected; described only through secondary sources).
  - Whether a typed instant message or email satisfies "signed" in s. 19(1) of the Copyright Act, 1957, including the role of electronic signatures under the IT Act.
  - Whether s. 30A imports the signature requirement of s. 19(1) into licences.
  - Whether accrued causes of action for infringement can be assigned, and how.
  - Delhi's pecuniary jurisdiction limits for district courts and the High Court, and therefore whether the Delhi High Court IP Division Rules, 2022 would apply.
  - Amendments to s. 12A of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015 after 2019, including any by the Mediation Act, 2023.
  - Section 13(2) and (3) of the Copyright Act, 1957 (conditions for subsistence).
  - Moral rights (s. 57 of the Copyright Act, 1957), for example a claim by the Photographer that the logo overlay distorted the Work.
  - The measure of damages in Indian copyright cases, including any decisions on licence-fee damages.
  - The subsequent treatment of every case cited, except where stated in the sources file.

## 3. Limitations of the method

- **Single author.** No second reviewer checked the analysis. In practice, a supervising lawyer would review every conclusion.
- **Status tags are judgments.** The line between [SOURCED] and [INFERENCE] is drawn by the author; another reader may draw it differently.
- **Risk levels are qualitative.** They summarise reasoning; they are not predictions and should not be read as probabilities.
- **Evidence protocol untested.** The protocol in `templates/evidence-handling-protocol.md` is written from published practice and statute. It has not been tested against a court's actual requirements for a s. 63(4) certificate.
- **Hash values.** No real evidence files exist, so no evidence hash is computed. The only computed hash in the repository is the worked example on the `.gitignore` file.
