## Element notes

### 1. Protected category
A photograph is an artistic work "whether or not" it possesses artistic quality (s. 2(c)(i)). Copyright subsists in original artistic works (s. 13(1)). The Work is a photograph [SOURCED] (E-03). Nothing in the scenario suggests the conditions in s. 13(2) about publication or the author's nationality are in doubt; I have not analysed that sub-section [ASSUMPTION].

### 2. Originality
The test applied in *Eastern Book Co.* (PARTIALLY VERIFIED) is skill and judgment with a minimal degree of creativity. The Work reflects the Photographer's choices of arrangement, light, timing and crop [STIPULATED]. The respondent's best point, that overhead food flat-lays are a common format, goes mainly to scope (Element 8). Conclusion: originality likely satisfied [INFERENCE].

### 3. Authorship
For a photograph, the author is "the person taking the photograph" (s. 2(d)(iv)). Supporting: E-03 (the Photographer delivered it as her frame); E-04 (capture date matches the shoot; camera serial matches the selects) [SOURCED]. Weakening: metadata can be edited, the file was re-saved on 2024-03-18, and no RAW exists on Quorvane's systems [SOURCED] (E-04). The Photographer's own evidence would close this gap [CONDITIONAL].

### 4. First ownership
The author is the first owner (s. 17), unless proviso (b) applies: a photograph taken for valuable consideration at the instance of a person belongs to that person "in the absence of any agreement to the contrary". Valuable consideration and instance are shown (E-01, E-02) [SOURCED]. But E-01 is an express agreement that the Photographer keeps copyright [SOURCED]. Contrast *The Cinema Resource Centre v. Ganga Rudraiah* (Mad HC, 2026), where the commissioning producer was held first owner without a written agreement and with no rival claimant; here there is a written contrary agreement [INFERENCE].

### 5. Standing
Quorvane can sue only as owner (by assignment) or as exclusive licensee (s. 54(a)). E-01's exclusive licence covers selects only; the Work was not a select (E-03) [SOURCED]. E-03's "use it as you like" and E-12's "treat it as yours" do not specify rights, duration or territory (s. 19(2)) [SOURCED]. Whether s. 30A imports the signature requirement of s. 19(1) into licences, and whether typed electronic messages satisfy it, are open questions for counsel [CONDITIONAL]. Practical fix: the Photographer's participation (see `enforcement-options.md`, Option 1).

### 6. Act restricted by copyright
Section 14(c) gives the exclusive right to reproduce the work in any material form, communicate it to the public and issue copies. The website banner (E-08) reproduces and communicates the Work [SOURCED]. The pouch (E-09) reproduces either the Work or a different photograph, which is the Element 8 question [CONDITIONAL]. E-07 shows a communication at an unknown date [SOURCED].

### 7. Without licence
Licence terms: E-06 email (12 months, India, owned digital channels; print and packaging excluded) [SOURCED]. Pebblequay's reliance on "ongoing" in the invoice [ALLEGATION] (E-10) and the possible argument that s. 19(5) via s. 30A deems five years if no period is stated [CONDITIONAL] are discussed in the memorandum, Issue 3. Packaging was never licensed on any reading [SOURCED].

### 8. Copying of a substantial part
Infringement needs copying, not mere similarity. *R.G. Anand* (VERIFIED) holds there is no copyright in ideas or themes, and frames the inquiry around whether the later work, viewed as a whole, gives an unmistakable impression of being a copy. Access is established: Pebblequay licensed the Work from August 2023 (E-06) [SOURCED]. For the website, the Work itself is used, so substantiality is not in issue [SOURCED]. For the pouch, see `comparative-work-analysis.md`: shared elements are mostly conventional; the most telling shared choice is the seven-piece crescent with the halved, pistachio-dusted piece at the lower end [INFERENCE].

### 9. Exceptions
Fair dealing under s. 52(1)(a) covers private or personal use including research, criticism or review, and reporting current events. Commercial website advertising and packaging do not fit those purposes [INFERENCE]. No other exception in s. 52 appears relevant on the facts; I have not reviewed every clause [ASSUMPTION].

### 10. Limitation
Article 88 gives three years from the date of infringement for compensation (PARTIALLY VERIFIED). Section 22 restarts time for a continuing tort (PARTIALLY VERIFIED). Whether continued display of a web image or continued sale of pouches is a continuing tort or a series of separate acts is a point for counsel [CONDITIONAL].
