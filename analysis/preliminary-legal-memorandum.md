## 4. Discussion

### Issue 1: Subsistence and originality

**Issue.** Is the Work an original artistic work in which copyright subsists?

**Relevant law.** Copyright subsists in original artistic works (Copyright Act, 1957, s. 13(1)). "Artistic work" includes a photograph, whether or not it has artistic quality (s. 2(c)(i)). For a photograph, the author is the person taking it (s. 2(d)(iv)). The Supreme Court in *Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak*, (2008) 1 SCC 1, applied a standard of skill and judgment with a minimal degree of creativity in the context of derivative works. I have only partly inspected that judgment's text; its holding is recorded as PARTIALLY VERIFIED. Copyright protects expression, not ideas, themes or facts (*R.G. Anand v. Delux Films*, (1978) 4 SCC 118, proposition 1).

**Application.** The Work is a photograph and so falls within s. 2(c)(i) [SOURCED]. The originality question is low-threshold for a staged photograph. The Photographer chose the crescent arrangement, the diagonal leaf, the brass plate's position, the halved laddoo with pistachio, the scattered petals, the light direction and the crop [STIPULATED]. Those are choices of skill and judgment that go beyond a mechanical record [INFERENCE]. Proof that the Photographer created it rests on E-03 (her email delivering it) and E-04 (metadata consistent with the shoot) [SOURCED].

**Counterarguments.** Pebblequay may argue that an overhead flat-lay of sweets on a banana leaf is a stock food-photography format, so originality is thin. That argument goes more to the scope of protection (Issue 4) than to subsistence. A weightier point is proof of authorship: the metadata in E-04 is editable, the file was re-saved in 2024, and the RAW file is missing [SOURCED].

**Evidence gaps.** RAW file; the Photographer's testimony about the shoot; any behind-the-scenes images or contact sheets from 11 February 2023.

**Preliminary conclusion.** Copyright very likely subsists in the Work [CONDITIONAL: on the Photographer confirming she took it]. **Risk: Low.** The protection may be narrow: it covers the particular combination of choices, not the subject or format.

### Issue 2: Ownership and standing

**Issue.** Who is the first owner of copyright in the Work, and can Quorvane sue?

**Relevant law.** The author is the first owner (s. 17), but under proviso (b) a photograph taken for valuable consideration at a person's instance belongs to that person "in the absence of any agreement to the contrary". An assignment must be in writing and signed (s. 19(1)), identify the work, rights, duration and territory (s. 19(2)), and defaults to five years and India where silent (s. 19(5), (6)). Licences follow the same formalities through ss. 30 and 30A. An exclusive licensee counts as owner for civil remedies (s. 54(a)). *The Cinema Resource Centre v. Ganga Rudraiah* (Mad HC, O.S.A. (CAD) No. 17 of 2022, 27 August 2026), VERIFIED on unofficial reproduction, applied proviso (b) to a producer who engaged photographers, despite the absence of a written agreement or payment proof, because no rival claim was made. It is persuasive in Delhi, not binding.

**Application.**
1. *Proviso (b) is engaged on its face.* The Photographer was paid (E-02) for a shoot at Quorvane's instance (E-01) [SOURCED].
2. *But there is an "agreement to the contrary".* E-01 records in writing that she keeps copyright and Quorvane accepted "on these terms" [SOURCED]. Unlike *Cinema Resource Centre*, where the court had no contrary agreement, here the contract points away from the commissioning party. On present evidence the Photographer is the first owner [INFERENCE].
3. *Quorvane's exclusive licence may not cover the Work.* The E-01 licence covers the "selects" and the catalogue. The Work was not a select (E-03) [SOURCED]. "Use it as you like" in E-03 could be read as (a) bringing the Work within the existing exclusive arrangement, (b) a separate non-exclusive licence, or (c) permission to use a copy without any right to license others [CONDITIONAL]. Only (a) would give Quorvane standing under s. 54(a), and (a) needs an exclusive grant in writing, which the email arguably lacks.
4. *E-12 does not cure the problem by itself.* It does not identify rights, duration or territory, so it does not meet s. 19(2) on its face [SOURCED]. Whether a typed instant message is "signed" under s. 19(1) is not something I have checked; it is listed for counsel. It is also silent on past infringements and the right to sue for them [SOURCED].
5. *The credit line cuts both ways.* The "© Quorvane Imaging LLP" credit (E-05) names the studio. The s. 55(2) presumption concerns a name appearing as author or publisher. Quorvane is not the author of a photograph it did not take, so the presumption is unlikely to help on authorship, and the credit may be put to Quorvane as an overstatement of its rights [INFERENCE].
6. *A knock-on issue for the licence.* If Quorvane had only a catalogue licence of selects, Pebblequay may argue that Quorvane had no authority to license the Work at all. That would not make Pebblequay's use lawful, since Pebblequay would then have no licence from the owner either, but it would reshape the claim and may bring the Photographer in as the proper claimant [CONDITIONAL].

**Counterarguments.** Quorvane may argue that E-01's "everything I shoot" was meant for the paid shoot generally, and that proviso (b) should apply to frames taken at its instance but not delivered as selects. That is weak against the plain written words. Quorvane may also argue that E-03 and E-12 together show the Photographer always treated the Work as Quorvane's to exploit. That helps on intention but does not supply the formalities.

**Evidence gaps.** The Photographer's written account of what she intended in E-03; any other messages between her and Quorvane in 2023; the exact terms of the "selects" licence.

**Preliminary conclusion.** On present evidence the Photographer is most likely the first owner, and Quorvane's standing as exclusive licensee of the Work is doubtful [CONDITIONAL]. **Risk: Significant.** The corrective step is to obtain the Photographer's cooperation. Two routes need counsel's advice: (a) a written assignment or exclusive licence of the Work that meets s. 19 and s. 30A and expressly deals with accrued causes of action; or (b) the Photographer joining as co-claimant. Route (b) avoids the open question whether accrued rights of action can be assigned, which I have not researched.

### Issue 3: Website banner and the scope of the licence

**Issue.** Was the banner seen on 16 January 2025 an unlicensed reproduction and communication to the public?

**Relevant law.** Copyright in an artistic work includes the exclusive right to reproduce it in any material form and to communicate it to the public (s. 14(c)). Infringement occurs when a person, without a licence from the owner, does anything the owner has the exclusive right to do (s. 51(a)(i)). The licence provisions are summarised under Issue 2. Where a licence does not state its period, s. 19(5), as applied by s. 30A, deems it to be five years.

**Application.**
1. *Use of the Work.* E-08 shows an image that appears identical to the Work on the home page, with Pebblequay's logo added [SOURCED]. Pebblequay does not deny using the Work on its website; it says the use was licensed (E-10) [ALLEGATION]. Use of the Work itself is therefore unlikely to be the real dispute [INFERENCE].
2. *Term.* The licence email states "12 months from 2023-08-01" and Pebblequay accepted it in writing (E-06) [SOURCED]. On that reading the licence ended on 31 July 2024 (EV-12) [CONDITIONAL].
3. *The invoice.* Pebblequay relies on "ongoing campaign" in the invoice (E-06, E-10) [ALLEGATION]. Quorvane's best reply is that the licence was formed by the offer email and the acceptance; the invoice is a billing document that refers back to that licence and does not purport to change it. "Ongoing campaign" more naturally describes Pebblequay's campaign than the licence term [INFERENCE].
4. *The s. 19(5) default.* If a court treated the invoice as the operative licence, Pebblequay could argue that no period was stated and that the five-year default applies, taking the licence to 2028 [CONDITIONAL]. The default should not arise where the period is stated in the documents that formed the licence. But this is the strongest point Pebblequay has on the website use, and it needs counsel's view.

**Counterarguments.** Beyond the invoice point, Pebblequay may argue implied extension: Quorvane did not complain until January 2025, and the "renewal" offer shows both sides expected a continuing relationship. Silence for nearly six months after expiry is unlikely to create a licence, but it may affect remedies and costs [INFERENCE].

**Evidence gaps.** Any capture between 1 August 2024 and 15 January 2025; whether the banner is still live; the image file and HTML from the website; the complete August 2023 email chain, including any drafts or calls.

**Preliminary conclusion.** On the documents, the website use after 31 July 2024 was probably outside the licence [CONDITIONAL: on the email and acceptance being treated as the licence and on Issue 2 being resolved]. **Risk: Moderate.**

### Issue 4: The undated social media post

**Issue.** Does E-07 evidence an infringing use?

**Relevant law.** As for Issue 3. The licence expressly covered "official social media accounts" (E-06).

**Application.** The post's date is unknown [SOURCED]. If it was posted between 1 August 2023 and 31 July 2024, it was licensed on any reading. If later, it may have been unlicensed, but continued display of an earlier licensed post after expiry raises its own question (whether the licence required removal at the end of the term), which E-06 does not address [CONDITIONAL]. ### Issue 5: The pouch image

**Issue.** Does the photograph on the Millrook pouch infringe the Work?

**Relevant law.** Reproduction and communication to the public are exclusive rights (s. 14(c)); dealing in infringing copies is a separate act (s. 51(b)). *R.G. Anand* (VERIFIED) holds that ideas and themes are unprotected, that a later work expressing the same idea differently is new, and that infringement requires an unmistakable impression of copying, proved by clear evidence (propositions 1, 3 to 6). It concerned a play and a film, so its use here is by analogy. *Temple Island Collections* (England and Wales, persuasive only, PARTIALLY VERIFIED) frames the question of recreated photographs and is contested as protecting ideas; I use it only to frame the question.

**Application.** There are two scenarios.

*Scenario A: the pouch reproduces the Work itself.* If the pouch image is a digital copy of the Work, however edited, the claim is straightforward on scope: packaging was expressly excluded (E-06) [SOURCED]. The comparison table in `analysis/comparative-work-analysis.md` shows features that tell against this scenario: a steel bowl instead of a brass plate, a mirrored leaf diagonal, a pouch in frame, light from the opposite side [ALLEGATION, partly SOURCED from E-09 and E-11]. If those differences are confirmed on the pouch itself, Scenario A is unlikely [CONDITIONAL].

*Scenario B: a reshoot.* Pebblequay had access to the Work for over a year before the asserted reshoot (E-06, E-11) [SOURCED]. The pouch shares the crescent of seven laddoos, the halved laddoo with pistachio at the lower end, and the overhead warm-toned treatment [SOURCED, from E-09]. Against that:
- laddoos on a banana leaf, an overhead flat-lay and a halved piece to show texture are common food-photography conventions, closer to ideas or stock elements than to protected expression (R.G. Anand, propositions 1 and 4) [INFERENCE];
- the differences listed above are real and visible [SOURCED];
- the asserted independent shoot (E-11) is plausible, though unproven [ALLEGATION].

The strongest similarity is the combination of the crescent of exactly seven and the halved, pistachio-dusted piece at the same end. A court might treat that combination as a deliberate echo of the Work's arrangement rather than coincidence [INFERENCE]. But a single shared arrangement in a common format is a thin basis for the "unmistakable impression" of copying.

**Counterarguments.** Independent creation: Pebblequay photographed its own product. Idea and expression: the shared features are generic. Clear evidence: Quorvane has one pouch and low-resolution reshoot copies.

**Evidence gaps.** A high-resolution scan of the pouch; the original files and RAW images of Pebblequay's shoot; the pouch artwork file; any brief given to the designer (in particular, whether the Work was used as a reference); sales period and volume.

**Preliminary conclusion.** **Risk: Indeterminate.** If Scenario A is established, the risk on scope becomes Low. If Scenario B, the claim is, on present evidence, closer to Significant risk, because the shared elements are largely conventional.

### Issue 6: Evidence law and proof

**Issue.** Which evidence statute governs, and how would the key electronic items be proved?

**Relevant law.** The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 applies from 1 July 2024. Section 170 repeals the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 but saves proceedings pending on that date (PARTIALLY VERIFIED, from reproductions). Electronic records are "documents" (s. 2(1)(d)) and are not inadmissible merely for being electronic, but admissibility is subject to s. 63. Under s. 63(4) a certificate in the Schedule form, signed by the person in charge of the device or activity, must accompany the record. In *Pune Bar Association v. Union of India* (W.P. (C) No. 599 of 2026, order of 22 May 2026), the Supreme Court declined to strike down s. 63(4), treated the hash requirement as a rational integrity safeguard, and indicated that a suitably skilled expert may sign Part B. The question of law was left open (PARTIALLY VERIFIED: order text on an unofficial database). *Arjun Panditrao Khotkar*, (2020) 7 SCC 1, held that the s. 65B(4) certificate is necessary under the 1872 Act, subject to the owner-in-the-witness-box route (PARTIALLY VERIFIED: operative conclusion only). Its transfer to s. 63 is unresolved.

**Application.** No suit is pending, so a suit filed now would likely fall under the 2023 Adhiniyam [CONDITIONAL]. E-08, captured with a log and hash at the time, is best placed for a s. 63(4) certificate [SOURCED]. Emails (E-01, E-03, E-06) should be exported in original form with headers and hashed now [INFERENCE]. E-04 is commentary on the JPEG, which is the evidence itself. E-07 would need its third-party source as witness, which is disproportionate for its weak content. E-09 can be produced as a physical item [INFERENCE].

**Counterarguments.** Pebblequay will challenge every item captured by Quorvane's own staff as self-serving. Independent or archival captures, and expert evidence, answer that challenge.

**Evidence gaps.** Original-format exports of the emails; device details for the s. 63(4) certificate; an expert for Part B; independent capture of current use.

**Preliminary conclusion.** **Risk: Moderate** overall on proof. It reduces to Low for the website use if the steps in `templates/evidence-handling-protocol.md` are followed promptly.

### Issue 7: Forum, pre-institution mediation, limitation and remedies

**Issue.** Where and how could a civil claim be brought, and what relief is realistic?

**Relevant law.** A suit lies in the district court where the plaintiff resides or carries on business (Copyright Act, 1957, s. 62). Copyright disputes are commercial disputes (Commercial Courts Act, 2015, s. 2(1)(c)(xvii)) if the specified value is at least ₹3 lakh (ss. 2(1)(i), 12(1)(d)). A commercial suit that does not contemplate urgent interim relief requires pre-institution mediation (s. 12A(1)), with a three-month period excluded from limitation (s. 12A(3)) (VERIFIED). *Patil Automation*, 2022 SCC OnLine SC 1028, held s. 12A mandatory (VERIFIED on the mandatory point; the effective date is from commentary). *Yamini Manohar*, (2024) 5 SCC 815, holds urgent-relief prayers must not be a disguise to avoid s. 12A (PARTIALLY VERIFIED). Limitation for infringement compensation is three years from infringement (Limitation Act, 1963, Art. 88), with s. 22 for continuing wrongs (PARTIALLY VERIFIED). Remedies are injunction, damages and accounts, with a knowledge-based limitation (Copyright Act, 1957, s. 55(1) and proviso) (VERIFIED).

**Application.**
1. *Forum.* Delhi is available under s. 62(2) because Quorvane and the Photographer are in Delhi [ASSUMPTION]. Sales in Delhi (E-09) may give an additional basis [INFERENCE].
2. *Commercial suit or ordinary suit.* The licence fee was ₹18,000 for a year (E-06) [SOURCED]. Damages for website use alone may be modest. Whether the claim reaches ₹3 lakh depends on how packaging use is valued, and that depends on Issue 5 [CONDITIONAL]. If it does not reach the specified value, the Commercial Courts Act framework, including s. 12A, would not apply in the usual way [CONDITIONAL]. Delhi's pecuniary limits for district and High Court jurisdiction were not checked.
3. *Mediation and urgency.* Quorvane knew of the uses by January 2025 and did nothing for about 20 months (EV-19 to EV-22) [STIPULATED]. A claim of urgency now would be open to the *Yamini Manohar* objection [INFERENCE]. Pre-institution mediation is in any case a sensible forum for a dispute whose most likely outcome is a retrospective licence fee.
4. *Limitation.* The earliest post-expiry use is 1 August 2024. Three years from then is 1 August 2027 [INFERENCE]. Continuing use may restart time under s. 22, but compensation for the earliest period should not be left to the last months [CONDITIONAL]. Any mediation period is excluded under s. 12A(3).
5. *Remedies.* The innocent-infringer proviso is unlikely to help Pebblequay: it took a licence of the Work and was told of the claim in January 2025 (E-06, E-10) [INFERENCE]. Damages are likely to be measured against a reasonable licence fee, which E-06 itself evidences [INFERENCE].

**Counterarguments.** Pebblequay will say the matter is low-value and that a suit is disproportionate. That is a fair point on the website use alone.

**Evidence gaps.** Pebblequay's packaging sales; Quorvane's rate card for print and packaging licences; whether use continues today.

**Preliminary conclusion.** **Risk: Moderate** on procedure, mainly because of the delay and the valuation uncertainty.

## 5. Overall conclusion

The case is weaker on title than on use. Pebblequay probably used the Work on its website after the licence ended, and its "ongoing" argument, while real, is answerable. But Quorvane cannot safely enforce anything until the Photographer's position is regularised, because the written terms of the engagement point to her as owner. The packaging claim is the most valuable and the least certain: everything depends on whether the pouch image is the Work or a reshoot, and a reshoot that shares only conventional elements may not infringe. The social media post should not be relied on.

Taken together: **Significant** risk on standing as matters stand, **Moderate** on the website claim once standing is fixed, and **Indeterminate** on packaging. Litigation is not justified on the present file. A documented, time-bounded approach for a retrospective licence is the proportionate first step.
