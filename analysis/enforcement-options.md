# Enforcement Options

> **FICTIONAL EXERCISE. NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** A graduated set of options, from least to most escalated. Costs are stated qualitatively: **Minimal** (internal time only), **Modest** (limited external spend), **Substantial** (counsel and expert fees over months), **High** (full litigation). No legal notice is drafted in this repository.

## Recommended sequence

Option 1 → Option 2 → Option 3 → Option 5, with Option 4 only if a dated post outside the licence is found. Option 6 is a fallback, not a plan. Reasons are given in the memorandum, section 5.

---

## Option 1: Further investigation and title clean-up

**What it involves.** Preserve evidence (G-03, G-04); obtain the Photographer's statement and RAW file (G-01, G-02); buy and scan further pouches (G-07); value the claim (G-10).

**Preconditions.** None. Can start immediately.

**Risks.** Low. The main risk is delay: the earliest damages period ends on 1 August 2027 under Article 88 (PARTIALLY VERIFIED). The Photographer may be unwilling to cooperate or may want a share of any recovery.

**Cost.** Minimal to Modest.

**Counsel's judgment needed on.** The form of any assignment or exclusive licence from the Photographer; whether accrued causes of action can be assigned and how; whether to join her instead.

---

## Option 2: Without-prejudice approach for a retrospective licence

**What it involves.** After Option 1, a short without-prejudice proposal to Pebblequay: a retrospective licence fee for website use from 1 August 2024, a forward licence if Pebblequay wants one, and a request for its pouch shoot files to resolve the reshoot question. Pebblequay has already offered to discuss renewal (E-10).

**Preconditions.** Title regularised or the Photographer participating (G-01). Evidence preserved. A fee position supported by Quorvane's rates (G-10).

**Risks.** Pebblequay may remove the banner and refuse payment. Disclosure of Quorvane's title difficulty would weaken its hand, so title must be fixed first. The communication must not overstate rights or make threats that cannot be backed.

**Cost.** Minimal to Modest.

**Counsel's judgment needed on.** Whether "without prejudice" protection applies to the communication as framed; the figure and structure of the proposal; whether to combine website and packaging claims or separate them.

---

## Option 3: Licensing or settlement discussion

**What it involves.** Negotiated agreement covering past use, future use (or removal), and the packaging question, possibly with a document exchange on the reshoot.

**Preconditions.** Engagement from Pebblequay following Option 2.

**Risks.** The packaging question may be the sticking point. A settlement should record that it is without admission if Pebblequay requires it, and should be signed by all rights holders, including the Photographer if she is the owner.

**Cost.** Modest.

**Counsel's judgment needed on.** Settlement terms, especially release scope and warranties of title from the Quorvane side.

---

## Option 4: Platform takedown (only if applicable)

**What it involves.** Using the social media platform's own copyright reporting process for a post shown to be live and outside the licence term.

**Preconditions.** A direct capture of the post with URL and date (G-06) showing it falls outside the licence. The complaint should be made by or with the authority of the owner.

**Legal context.** Under the Information Technology Act, 2000, s. 79, intermediaries have safe harbour subject to conditions, including acting on "actual knowledge". As amended in 2025, rule 3(1)(d) of the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 ties actual knowledge for its purposes to a court order or a reasoned written intimation from an authorised government officer (PARTIALLY VERIFIED). For copyright, the Delhi High Court in *MySpace Inc. v. Super Cassettes Industries Ltd.* (Division Bench, 23 December 2016) is reported to have accepted that a rights holder's notice identifying specific content and locations can give an intermediary the knowledge relevant to copyright liability (PARTIALLY VERIFIED). The interaction between that decision and the 2025 amendment is not settled in my research. In practice, the platform's own policy process is the realistic route.

**Risks.** A takedown of a post that was actually licensed would be wrongful and damaging to the relationship. The website and the pouch are not platform-hosted user content; takedown does not reach them.

**Cost.** Minimal.

**Counsel's judgment needed on.** Whether a takedown request is consistent with an ongoing negotiation; who signs it.

---

## Option 5: Pre-institution mediation (Commercial Courts Act, 2015, s. 12A)

**What it involves.** Applying to the authority notified under s. 12A for mediation before any commercial suit.

**Preconditions.** The dispute must be a commercial dispute of specified value (at least ₹3 lakh) for the Commercial Courts Act framework to apply. That depends on the packaging valuation (G-09, G-10). Mediation must be completed within three months, extendable by two with consent, and the period is excluded from limitation (s. 12A(3)).

**Risks.** Pebblequay may not engage. A settlement reached has the status of an arbitral award on agreed terms (s. 12A(5)), so the terms must be right.

**Cost.** Modest.

**Counsel's judgment needed on.** Valuation; whether the claim is commercial; whether amendments to s. 12A after 2019 (including any made by the Mediation Act, 2023) change the procedure. I have not checked them.

---

## Option 6: Civil proceedings and interim relief

**What it involves.** A suit for injunction, damages and accounts under s. 55(1) of the Copyright Act, 1957, in Delhi under s. 62(2), as a commercial suit or an ordinary civil suit depending on valuation.

**Preconditions.** Title regularised; evidence preserved and certifiable under s. 63(4) BSA; s. 12A complied with unless urgent interim relief is genuinely contemplated; claim within limitation.

**Risks.**
- **Urgency:** after about 20 months of inaction (EV-21 to EV-22), a claim of urgency invites the *Yamini Manohar* objection that urgent relief is a disguise to avoid s. 12A (PARTIALLY VERIFIED), and an interim injunction is unlikely on delay grounds alone [INFERENCE].
- **Standing:** if title is not fixed, the suit may fail at the threshold (memo Issue 2).
- **Packaging:** if the pouch is an independent reshoot, the most valuable part of the claim may fail on similarity (memo Issue 5).
- **Proportionality:** costs may exceed recovery on the website claim alone.

**Cost.** High.

**Counsel's judgment needed on.** Whether to sue at all; forum and valuation; whether to seek interim relief; pleadings; expert evidence.

---

## Options not recommended

- **Criminal complaint.** Criminal remedies under the Copyright Act, 1957 are outside the scope of this project. On these facts, a dispute about licence term and an arguable reshoot is a civil matter.
- **Public statements or social media call-outs.** Risk of defamation and of undermining settlement; no enforcement benefit.
- **Test purchases using a false identity, or any approach to Pebblequay staff under a pretext.** Not to be done without legal advice; ordinary retail purchases are sufficient here.
